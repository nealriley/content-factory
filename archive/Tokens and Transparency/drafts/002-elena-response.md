# Response: Alternatives to Opaque Credits

## Elena does a great job of highlighting the challenges
Credits are a necessary evil while LLM costs are high, and Elena Verna is right that credits can keep usage from spiking out of control and bankrupting vendors. Lovable treats credits as a cost to shrink, celebrating 20–30% reductions in per-action credits by tightening prompts and codifying workflows instead of squeezing customers. That is the promise of metered billing done right: when the meter is visible, customers can adapt behavior to cut costs, and vendors can improve efficiency to lower burn without hiding the math. But the same model goes wrong when credits hide true compute costs and every invoice feels unfair because value and price only meet in hindsight.

## There are many ways to buy and sell tokens
Hybrid billing lands best with a base subscription that guarantees a revenue floor while tokens meter variable-heavy features; finance teams can forecast, and customers still see how usage drives cost. Prepaid token packs paired with a light platform fee give procurement a clear budget envelope while keeping room for spiky workloads; rollover or quarterly expiry signals how much runway is left. Commit-to-consume plans price tokens against expected use for the year, then true up quarterly; buyers get discounts for predictability while the vendor locks in baseline revenue. When your pricing is static your only competition becomes changes to your product or experience; with metered billing the vendor has flexibility, including lower-cost options a user can switch between to meet an optimization gap.

## Transparency and clarity of tokens and their usage is key
When you monetize the underlying function with credits instead of pure access, spell out which actions burn units; clear meter definitions are the only way to build trust and keep customer relationships steady. Transparency requires two main foundations: define and design the units of measure of your product, and find ways to easily expose the right view of this information to your end users. Expose the meter unit in-product, show real-time balance or burn, publish the math on invoices, and let buyers self-forecast before they commit, so they can change or adapt their use without guesswork.

## Predictability comes from stable consumption, which is difficult in this AI environment
Predictability is built on some level of commitment: minimum spend or pre-paying for access ties revenue to capacity, especially for AI features that rely on hardware. Committed spend puts the relationship into one where the customer tries to find value as the cost is sunk—healthy when customers actively seek value, toxic when they demand proof after the fact. Investing in lower-cost infrastructure can reduce cost per user, but it competes with R and D for new flagship models; the tradeoff is optimizing today’s value versus building for tomorrow’s interaction model. Unlike prior tech cycles, the intelligence age sits atop still-maturing industrial and information ages, so predicting usage and its rate is markedly more difficult, which makes any transparent meter plus a predictable anchor even more important.
