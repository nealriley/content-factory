Brief:
- Audience: product and finance leads wrestling with AI metered pricing.
- Goal: explain why token-based billing is hard to forecast and trust.
- Promise: a three-paragraph narrative that surfaces the friction and points to mitigations without hiding the meter.

Token billing looks scientific but hides moving parts: tokenization differs across providers, inputs and outputs carry different rates, and vendors can quietly swap tokenizers or models. The same 100 words can tokenize differently and skew cost, so buyers can’t make apples-to-apples comparisons or sanity-check value. Credits add another opaque layer—one vendor maps a credit to a token, another to a thousand, another to “slow” usage—making the monetizable unit drift away from the value customers receive.

Volatility compounds the difficulty. Model costs and competitive pricing change constantly, and creative outputs swing widely in length, so even a predictable workflow can burn more or less than yesterday. Vendors hedge by blending models or capping free trials, but that can feel like rationing instead of value when users don’t see what’s being consumed. Without clear receipts, support turns into triage, explaining surprise burn rates rather than product outcomes.

The only workable path is to expose the meter and pair it with guardrails. Show live token tallies, highlight the input/output gap, and let buyers set caps and budgets so they can dial usage up or down. Use token pricing early, when consumption risk is high and control matters, then transition stable customers to seats or outcomes once usage patterns settle. Transparency and predictable mappings keep token billing usable, even when the unit itself is messy.
